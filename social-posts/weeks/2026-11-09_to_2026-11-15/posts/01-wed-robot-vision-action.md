## Source notes

- [Google DeepMind: Gemini Robotics 2.5](https://deepmind.google/blog/gemini-robotics-2-5-accelerates-real-world-physical-intelligence/), published 10 November 2026, checked 11 November 2026. Google reports a proof-of-concept with Apptronik and Mercedes-Benz in which Apollo 2 sorted automotive brackets from bins into ordered trays. Its embodied reasoning model tracked the workflow; Gemini Robotics 2.5 controlled the hands and arms. Google says the model is rolling out to robotics partners.
- [Google DeepMind: Gemini Robotics models](https://deepmind.google/models/gemini-robotics/) describes Gemini Robotics 2.5 as a vision-language-action model that converts vision and language inputs into motor control. The see/instruction/action sequence is a simplified mental model, not a claim that the real system runs exactly three isolated steps. This post presents the company's claims, without independent testing.
- Google reports a 96% success rate for the pilot, but the post deliberately omits that figure because it cannot establish the full test conditions from the announcement. A specific pilot does not establish performance in every environment.

But how does it help a robot actually pick one up?

Imagine a bin full of metal parts. A robot needs to pick one part and place it in a tray.

Sounds simple. But the parts may be in different positions, at different angles, or partly hidden by other parts.

𝐇𝐞𝐫𝐞'𝐬 𝐭𝐡𝐞 𝐛𝐚𝐬𝐢𝐜 𝐢𝐝𝐞𝐚:

👀 See: A camera provides images of the bin and its surroundings.

💬 Understand the task: An instruction tells the system what needs to be done.

🤖 Act: The AI model turns visual information and instructions into actions that guide the robot's arm and hand.

This kind of system is called a vision-language-action model. The name describes its inputs and output: images + instructions → robot actions.

The diagram is simplified. A real robot also needs hardware and a system to coordinate the work.

Google DeepMind says its Gemini Robotics models helped an Apptronik Apollo 2 robot sort automotive parts in a Mercedes-Benz factory pilot. One model tracked the workflow; Gemini Robotics 2.5 guided the robot's hands and arms.

It's a promising pilot, not proof that robots can do every physical task. Google says the model is rolling out to robotics partners.
