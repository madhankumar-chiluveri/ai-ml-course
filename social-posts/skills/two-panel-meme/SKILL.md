# Two-Panel Meme

Tell one relatable joke through a setup in the top half and a punchline in the bottom half.
Both halves must be complete scenes with their own readable text. This is one image, not a carousel.

## Establish the joke

Read [engineering-humour](../engineering-humour/SKILL.md) for the technical premise and evidence boundary.
Follow [style-ref-editorial](../style-ref-editorial/SKILL.md) for Madhan's visual system, Mad references, and export preparation.

When adapting a reference, inspect the actual image or post before claiming a match.
Identify its situation, audience tension, and joke mechanism. Preserve those three elements while creating original wording and artwork.
If the reference is inaccessible, ask for its screenshot or missing details; do not invent its contents from a title or URL.

Lock the exact setup and punchline before production. Keep one comic mechanism and let the second half deliver the payoff.
Match the reference's comic intensity as well as its topic. Use a visible reversal, scale change, physical escalation, or reaction when that carries the joke; a calm before/after illustration may explain the situation without being funny.
Verify technical or product claims with an appropriate source. Check any production analogy against the real mechanism.
Treat invented scenes and dialogue as fiction; do not imply a real incident, quote, measurement, or personal experience.

## Compose one filled image

- Use one 4:5 canvas, exported at exactly **2160 × 2700 px**. Divide it horizontally into two equal **2160 × 1350 px** panels.
- Fill each half with a coherent scene and purposeful background. Avoid an empty half or text floating outside its scene.
- Put the setup visibly inside the top half and the punchline visibly inside the bottom half. Keep both complete at feed size.
- Use a clear divider and a top-to-bottom reading order. Keep dialogue, figures, and essential props inside their panel boundaries.
- Default to image generation for the complete cohesive image. Specify exact wording, panel geometry, and shared character identity in the prompt.
- Use Madhan's warm paper, ink, and pastel palette. Keep `madvibe.ai` quiet and readable. Omit visible dates unless explicitly requested.
- Choose normal-width, moderately bold type with open counters and clear letter and word spacing. Reject touching glyphs, merged words, condensed heavy headings, and text collisions.

Mad and supporting characters must perform the joke through their gaze, expression, pose, and contact with props.
Use the existing Mad artwork as an identity reference and adapt the outfit when it serves the situation.
Single-image panels are not carousel pages: the carousel character-frequency rule does not prohibit characters in both halves.
Use both halves for character scenes when that makes the setup and payoff clearer.

## Validate the asset

Inspect the full image and both halves at roughly 360 px feed width.
Read every word; verify spelling, word spaces, dialogue attribution, technical labels, and setup/punchline order.
Trace each visible arm from shoulder through elbow and wrist to its hand. Reject duplicate or detached limbs and incorrect hands.
Check gaze, expression, prop contact, speech-bubble tails, panel alignment, cropping, and the exact export dimensions.
Regenerate faulty artwork or text rather than hiding errors with an unrelated overlay.
Apply the linked editorial skill's PNG preparation and verify that it preserves the decoded pixels.

## Deliver the requested mode

An idea or brief returns the situation, mechanism, exact panel text, scene actions, and evidence boundary; it does not create a finished image.
For production, use the next unused stable `LI-###` ID and the existing post template.
Deliver one final PNG, its native generated source and prompt, concise alt text, and one canonical caption per platform.
Set both platform formats to `single_image`. Keep the LinkedIn and Instagram captions brief so the image delivers the joke.
Store each caption once in the post file; include short source notes only when useful.
This skill does not authorize publishing, scheduling, messaging, or other account actions.
