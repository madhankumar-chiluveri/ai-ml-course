# Topic and Theme Router

Use this router after identifying one audience, one idea, and one desired reader action. Position every post as evidence-led **AI Product Engineering** for full-stack engineers moving toward reliable AI systems.

## Route in this order

1. Name the engineering question: failure, decision, result, build, lesson, or timely change.
2. Identify the strongest available evidence: experiment, trace, runnable demo, code/architecture, cited source, or first-hand observation.
3. Identify the information shape: one result, sequence, branching choice, live behavior, or concise argument.
4. Select the archetype, then the format. Never choose a carousel, video, or visual merely for predicted reach.

## Routing matrix

| Topic and evidence | Archetype / theme | Default format | Change format when |
|---|---|---|---|
| Benchmark, evaluation, math behavior, or measured comparison | AI Lab Notebook | Single-image result card | Use carousel only when the method and limitations need a sequence; use video for a changing trace or live experiment. |
| Agent, RAG, MCP, memory, or product architecture decision | Production Blueprint | Single-image diagram | Use carousel for a decision tree, teardown, or multi-stage flow that is unreadable on one card. |
| Failure, incident, bad output, debugging session, or reliability lesson | Production AI Failure File | Single-image evidence card | Use video when reproduction or trace playback is the proof; carousel for evidence-to-root-cause-to-fix progression. |
| Working feature, prototype, integration, or before/after behavior | Product Proof | Native video | Use one image when the proof is a static output or comparison; add text only when the demonstration is already self-explanatory. |
| Changed belief, course insight, mistake, or career transition | Engineering Field Notes | Text | Add one image only when a diagram, artifact, or result materially strengthens the lesson. |
| Named product/model/tool change with a practical engineering implication | Technical Analysis Brief | Text or single-image comparison | Use carousel only for several dependent decisions; use video only to demonstrate changed behavior. |

## Format rules

- Prefer **single image** for one mental model, measured result, comparison, or architecture card. Consult the project's latest audit before claiming an account-specific advantage.
- Use **document/carousel** only when order, progression, branching, or several necessary panels carry meaning. Do not claim a reach advantage without current account evidence.
- Use **text** for timely judgment, personal learning, or a concise field note. Compare medians as well as averages when the account has an outlier.
- Use **native video** for a 45–60 second demo, reproduction, or trace walkthrough. Test at least three comparable videos before inferring an advantage.
- Use a **poll** only when each option represents a real engineering trade-off and the answer will inform later analysis.

## Tie-breakers

- Choose the smallest format that preserves the reasoning and proof.
- When evidence is weak, narrow or qualify the claim; do not compensate with visual polish.
- Connect foundation topics explicitly to latency, cost, reliability, evaluation, security, developer experience, or product behavior.
- Vary weekly formats and goals, but never at the expense of fit.
- If no route fits, classify the information shape first; do not invent a new visual identity for one post.

## Prohibited routing shortcuts

- Do not imitate a named creator or “top 1% influencer” voice or visual style.
- Do not force an analogy, infographic, carousel, hashtag count, character range, or hook formula.
- Do not promise virality, impressions, or follower growth.
- Do not present credentials, experience, results, or experiments the author does not have.
